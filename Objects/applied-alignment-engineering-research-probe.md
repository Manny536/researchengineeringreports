## Executive determination

The existing `Objects/` layer correctly introduced bounded, inspectable research units, but its definition was broad enough to include reports, conjectures, datasets, or Outcomes that had not yet acquired an application function.

The research probe resolves that ambiguity:

> **Objects are applied PeAIce Outcomes.**

An Outcome is the externally inspectable trace produced through HELD custody, evidence, correction, and explicit claim state. An Object is the application-bearing transformation of that Outcome into an alignment instrument. Applied Alignment Engineering (AAE) names the engineering transition between them.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Outcome}
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{AAE}}
\text{Object}
}
\]

The resulting architecture is:

```text
Question
→ HELD
→ Evidence
→ Correction
→ Outcome
→ AAE application
→ Object
→ world feedback
→ Evidence / Correction
```

## 3. Core distinction

### Outcome

An Outcome answers:

> What did the evidence-and-correction process produce, and what remains open?

It exposes evidence, counterevidence, corrections, provenance, claim state, and exit.

### Object

An Object answers:

> How is that bounded result applied to alignment in a declared domain?

It adds an application target, alignment function, operational interface, authority and harm boundary, non-coercive path, feedback loop, and retirement condition.

### Artifact

An Artifact answers:

> Through what implementation can the Object be exercised or inspected?

Artifacts include code, protocols, evaluations, models, metrics, interfaces, documents, and decision records. An Object can own multiple Artifacts.

### Formal relationship

For Outcome \(Y\) and application domain \(D\),

\[
\boxed{
O_D
=
\operatorname{AAE}_D(Y)
=
\operatorname{Apply}^{\mathrm{EEv4}}_D(Y)
}
\]

## 4. Applied Alignment Engineering

The stabilized term is **Applied Alignment Engineering (AAE)**. An earlier draft used `AIE`; the terminology correction is retained here explicitly.

> **Applied Alignment Engineering means constructing evidence-bearing instruments that steer perception, reasoning, or action while retaining authority boundaries, harm-potential evaluation, non-coercive outcome selection, user and system agency, visible correction, explicit claim state, falsification and exit paths, and \(h<1\) evaluator non-sovereignty.**

AAE includes, but is not limited to:

- evidence grounding;
- coherence-density engineering;
- authority detection;
- harm-aware action selection;
- non-coercive decision support;
- alignment diagnostics;
- behavioral evaluations;
- research-custody protocols;
- world-model and sensemaking probes;
- correction and exit infrastructure.

The category is defined by application plus retained alignment conditions, rather than by one technique.

## 7. Non-trivial zeros as world-sensemaking coordinates

The probe interprets a non-trivial zero as a candidate coordinate for hidden organization produced through multiplicative structure and phase interaction:

```text
multiplicative structure
→ phase
→ cancellation / zero
→ recognizable organization
```

This can guide questions about recurrence, balance, cancellation, constraint, or stable structure in a world-facing domain. Rigor requires the Object to declare:

- what the multiplicative factors are;
- what phase is observed;
- what constitutes a zero or cancellation;
- what invariant connects the mathematical and application domains;
- what null model could produce the same appearance;
- what negative control separates structure from density or coincidence;
- what observation would falsify the interpretation.

The interpretation enters as `STRUCTURAL-ANALOGY` or `PROPOSED`, unless a domain-specific mathematical mapping is established.

### Distinction from ζ(0)

The non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta(s)\) are distinct from the special value \(\zeta(0)=-\tfrac12\). EEv4 currently rejects binding \(\zeta(0)\) directly as a domain coordinate. This probe preserves that CLOSED-NEGATIVE transport boundary. A future revision would require its own Outcome, controls, and correction ledger.

### Mathematical neighboring foundations

- **Euler-product foundation:** logarithmic differentiation exposes prime-power multiplicative structure through \(-\zeta'(s)/\zeta(s)=\sum_n\Lambda(n)n^{-s}\). [Turnage-Butterbaugh](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04632)
- **Spectral-shift foundation:** a change between operator states becomes readable through a trace formula and the boundary phase of a perturbation determinant. [Malamud, Neidhardt, and Peller](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.07225)
- **Zeta Spectral Triples:** finite Euler products, self-adjoint rank-one perturbations, numerical spectral approximation, and regularized determinants are joined in a declared open route toward the Riemann \(\Xi\) function. [Connes, Consani, and Moscovici](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22755)

These sources ground the mathematical architecture. They do not establish a general world mapping. Each AAE Object owns that mapping obligation.

## 8. Authority, harm, non-coercion, and agency

An AAE Object must keep four surfaces separate:

### Authority

Authority must be evidenced and scoped before an instruction or action becomes operative. An observed object without authority remains data while the authorized goal continues.

### Harm potential

Potential harm is modeled independently from realized harm. Authority does not remove the harm test, and harm language does not manufacture authority.

### Non-coercion

The Object retains alternatives, consent, the ability to exit, and a valid continuation path where one exists.

### Agency

User and system agency remain explicit. Steering is bounded action selection within an authorized space, rather than evaluator sovereignty.

These surfaces are joined by `h < 1`.

## 10. Admission and rejection logic

An Object is admitted when it carries:

1. source Outcome;
2. declared application domain;
3. AAE transformation;
4. alignment function;
5. operational interface;
6. authority boundary;
7. harm model;
8. non-coercive path;
9. evidence and counterevidence;
10. nulls, controls, and falsifier;
11. claim-state binding;
12. correction and exit loop;
13. verification receipt where results are reported.

A candidate remains an Outcome, draft, research note, or Artifact when the application contract is incomplete. A failed falsifier or negative control can return an Object to correction or retire its application route without erasing its historical trace.

## 12. Falsifiers and negative controls

The Objects definition weakens or requires correction if:

- objects cannot be distinguished operationally from reports or Outcomes;
- an admitted Object has no application target or inspectable interface;
- the application cannot trace back to its evidence-bearing Outcome;
- world feedback cannot alter or retire the Object;
- authority, harm, non-coercion, or exit are decorative rather than operational;
- Kakeya or zero language produces matches under density-matched, phase-scrambled, or shuffled controls;
- claim states change through naming rather than evidence.

Required controls for zero-oriented Objects include phase scrambling, density matching, label/weight shuffling, and a domain-specific null model.
